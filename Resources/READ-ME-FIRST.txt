HeatBox: read me first
======================

HeatBox saves videos from the web to your Mac in the quality you
choose, keeps a library of them, and converts files you already have. It has
no accounts and collects nothing.

You need a Mac with macOS 13 or later (Apple silicon or Intel). This version
was run on macOS 26 and 27 on Apple silicon. It was not run on macOS 13 to 15,
or on an Intel Mac, before release.


1. INSTALL
   HeatBox used to be called Studio x Phobos. If you have that app, quit it
   and move the old Studio x Phobos app from your Applications folder to the
   Trash before installing HeatBox. Your library, settings, queue and browser
   buttons are kept: both names use the same data, which is why only one of
   them should stay on your Mac.

   Drag HeatBox into your Applications folder.


2. OPEN IT THE FIRST TIME
   macOS will refuse at first, because the app is not signed with an Apple
   Developer ID. This is expected.
   - Open the app. A warning appears. Close it. Do not move the app to the Trash.
   - Open System Settings > Privacy & Security and scroll down.
   - Press "Open Anyway" beside the message about HeatBox, and confirm.
   You do this once for each new version.

   To check that your download is the one that was published, compare its
   fingerprint with the .sha256 file next to it:
       shasum -a 256 -c HeatBox-<version>-macos-universal.zip.sha256


3. ONE-TIME SETUP
   The first time, the app says it needs three free programs (yt-dlp, FFmpeg
   and Deno). Press "Install the Programs". The app downloads them, checks
   each against a fixed fingerprint, and keeps them in its own folder. If you
   would rather use Homebrew, the same screen shows the command.


4. USE IT
   Copy the address of a video page, open the app, press Paste, choose a
   version, and press Download. Videos go to your Movies folder, in a folder
   named after the website. Change that in Settings.


5. MEMBERS-ONLY VIDEOS (optional)
   The app can use your own YouTube sign-in for videos you may watch. In
   Settings, choose your browser and press "Copy my sign-in". If macOS blocks
   it, open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Full Disk Access, switch
   the app on, then quit and reopen it. The saved sign-in works like a
   password and stays on your Mac. Switch it off when you are not using it.


6. A FAIR WARNING
   Saving a video can be against a website's terms, and what you may do with
   a copy depends on where you live and who made it. Use the app for your own
   viewing of things you are entitled to watch.
